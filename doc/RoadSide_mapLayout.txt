Based strictly on the text of the novel Roadside Picnic, there are no real-world GPS coordinates, street addresses, or compass bearings. However, the Strugatsky brothers provide a clear relative spatial sequence for both the global phenomenon and the local Harmont map so you can lay out the landmarks in exact order.

1. Global Positioning: The Pilman Radiant

In the prologue, Dr. Valentine Pilman explains that all six Visitation Zones form a smooth, continuous curve across the globe (corresponding to a point between Earth and the star Deneb in the constellation Cygnus).
In the text, the only explicitly named global coordinates/regions along this curve are:
• Harmont (an unnamed English-speaking country—implied to be Canada via dollars, Royal Armoured Corps, and local slang—located on the edge of the primary Zone).
• Five other unnamed Zones distributed along a single smooth planetary trajectory (the novel itself does not name Siberia, Uganda, or the South Pacific; those come from secondary adaptations).

2. Local Map: Harmont to the Core (In Spatial Order)

Every location in the book sits along a linear progression starting from the safe town center, crossing the border, and moving deeper into the Harmont Zone.

A. Outside the Zone (The Town of Harmont)

1. The "Clean" Districts / New Harmont: The outer residential areas where evacuated residents and UN personnel live safely away from the border.
2. The Plague Quarter: The dilapidated, abandoned neighborhood immediately bordering the Zone where people refused to evacuate (and where strange mutations/resurrections occur).
3. The Borscht (The Gutter): A seedy tavern in Harmont located near the edge of town, serving as the primary meeting spot for stalkers and black-market buyers.
4. The International Institute for Extraterrestrial Cultures: A 13-story research building situated directly on the boundary line. Its upper floors have thick leaded-glass windows looking directly out over the entire Zone.
5. The Perimeter / Cordon: The heavily fortified boundary line separating Harmont from the Zone, consisting of barbed wire, concrete walls, searchlights, and armed UN guard posts.

B. Inside the Zone (Outer Rim to Center)

6. The Abandoned Suburbs / Old Town: Just inside the perimeter; empty, crumbling houses and overgrown yards left behind after the Visitation.
7. The Old Factory / Industrial Ruins: A decaying industrial sector on the near side of the Zone filled with rusted machinery, trenches, and early anomalies (such as "mosquito balds" and "hell slime").
8. The Highway / Asphalt Road: A broken, cracked road running through the middle of the Zone that stalkers use as a primary navigational landmark and path for their "boot" (all-terrain vehicle).
9. The Garage: A ruined transport depot along the road inside the Zone, used by Redrick Schuhart as a mid-way staging point and hiding spot.
10. The Marsh / Swamp: A treacherous, wet lowland area deeper inside the Zone, notorious for invisible gravity traps and pools of lethal "witches' jelly."
11. The Hills / Ridge: Elevated terrain beyond the marsh that overlooks the innermost basin of the Zone.
12. The Quarry (The Core): A massive, deep excavation pit at the far end of the Zone. It is guarded by the invisible "Meat Grinder" anomaly at its entrance and houses the Golden Sphere at its center.

Mapping Tip for the Book's Layout

If you are drawing this on a flat map:
• Place Harmont on one side, separated by a hard boundary line (The Perimeter) with The Institute sitting right on that border.
• Draw the Harmont Zone as a relatively compact area (the text notes you can see almost the entire Zone from the 13th floor of the Institute).
• Run a broken road from the Perimeter past the Old Factory and Garage, curving around the Marsh and leading straight to The Quarry at the far back edge.
