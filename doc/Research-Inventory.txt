To build a simulation based on Roadside Picnic, you need collectible artifacts ("swag"), stationary environmental hazards ("anomalies"), systemic zone phenomena, and stalker mechanics.

1. Artifacts (Collectible Items)

Artifacts are physical objects left behind by the Visitors. Some have practical human applications, while others are incomprehensible or lethal.
• So-So (Etak / Batteries): Small black cylinders or discs that generate endless electrical energy and self-replicate under certain conditions; used to power vehicles and devices.
• Empty (Hydraulic / Spacers): Two copper-colored discs separated by ~18 inches of empty space that resist any force trying to push them together or pull them apart.
• Full Empty: An Empty containing a viscous, luminescent blue liquid inside the gap; far rarer and heavier than a standard Empty.
• Bracelets: Metallic rings that emit radiation or stimulate biological processes, boosting human health and metabolism.
• Black Sparks (Black Sprays): Black beads or droplets that absorb and delay light, creating a visual lag when moved or illuminated.
• Pins & Needles: Small crystalline or metallic objects that emit faint colored flashes or magnetic pulses; their true purpose is unknown.
• Itchers: Objects that induce severe psychological distress, panic, and aggressive barking or howling behavior in dogs and humans nearby.
• Wriggling Magnet: A constantly vibrating, oscillating magnetic object.
• Spinning Ring (Perpetual Ring): A white metal ring that spins endlessly at high speed on any flat surface without friction loss.
• Sponges: Porous objects capable of absorbing and neutralizing certain Zone toxins or radiation.
• Lobster Eyes: Glassy, iridescent spheres with unknown optical properties.
• Rattling Napkins / White Ring: Strange, non-reactive curiosities with no discovered utility.
• Death Lamp: A cylindrical device that emits invisible rays lethal to all biological organisms within range.
• Golden Sphere (Golden Ball): A heavy, warm, golden sphere located deep in the Zone that telepathically reads a person's deepest, truest subconscious desire and grants it—requiring a human sacrifice to bypass its guardian anomaly. [1] (https://pid.bungie.org/Roadside_Picnic_Artifacts.html), [2] (https://stalker.fandom.com/wiki/Roadside_Picnic)

2. Anomalies (Localized Hazards)

Anomalies are stationary or semi-stationary pockets where the laws of physics break down. [1] (https://scholarworks.calstate.edu/downloads/sf268585f), [2] (https://stalker.fandom.com/wiki/Roadside_Picnic)
• Mosquito Mange ("Graviconcentrate"): An invisible pocket of extreme gravity (thousands of Gs) that crushes anything entering it into a flat, dense pancake. [1] (https://stalker.fandom.com/wiki/Roadside_Picnic)
• Meatgrinder: An invisible, lethal spatial distortion that violently twists, compresses, and tears apart any matter inside it; after triggering, it goes dormant briefly before recharging. [1] (https://stalker.fandom.com/wiki/Roadside_Picnic)
• Hell Slime (Witch's Jelly): A corrosive, glowing fluid found in basements and low-lying areas that dissolves organic tissue and solid matter on contact, turning bones into rubbery jelly; can be collected in specialized ceramic/glass containers. [1] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_C8yAeVWCxY&t=666), [2] (https://pid.bungie.org/Roadside_Picnic_Artifacts.html)
• Burning Fuzz: A plant-like or fibrous growth on metal and wires that ignites or burns on contact (note: on r/stalker, fans point out that the novel treats the fibrous plant material and a separate late-book burning heat-haze anomaly as distinct phenomena). [1] (https://www.reddit.com/r/stalker/comments/1g9quwr/i_created_a_simple_document_to_categorize_all/)
• Silver Web: Delicate, nearly invisible metallic threads hanging across paths; touching them causes delayed, fatal cardiac arrest.
• Jolly Ghosts: Localized pockets of swirling air, light, or sudden thermal/kinetic bursts that disorient or burn trespassers. [1] (https://pid.bungie.org/Roadside_Picnic_Artifacts.html)
• Shadows / Time-Lags: Zones where shadows point toward the sun or where time and gravity fluctuate unpredictably.
• Greenish / Blueish Lightning & Fire: Spontaneous, localized electrical discharges and cold or intense flames. [1] (https://pid.bungie.org/Roadside_Picnic_Artifacts.html), [2] (https://stalker.fandom.com/wiki/Roadside_Picnic)

3. Systemic Zone Mechanics & Simulation Rules

To make a simulation feel authentic to the book rather than just a generic shooter, incorporate these core world rules:
• Nuts & Bolts (Detection): Most anomalies are invisible. Stalkers throw metal nuts and bolts ahead of themselves to test gravity, air density, and trigger thresholds before stepping forward.
• Day vs. Night Cycle: Stalkers infiltrate at night to avoid UN/military patrols, searchlights, and automated turrets, relying on infrared goggles, night vision, and sensory intuition.
• Containment & Economy: Artifacts are smuggled out past military cordons and sold either legally to the International Institute for Extraterrestrial Cultures or illegally on the black market via middlemen.
• The "Emigration" Effect (Long-Term Debuffs): Anyone who lives near a Zone and moves away triggers localized disasters, bad luck, and severe statistical anomalies wherever they relocate.
• Genetic Mutation: Prolonged Zone exposure does not give stalkers superpowers; instead, their children develop non-human traits (such as Red's daughter, "Monkey," who grows thick golden fur and loses human speech).
• The "Living Dead" (Replicas): Deceased people buried near the Zone sometimes rise as autonomous, mindless biological replicas that walk back to their former homes.
• Protective Gear: Specialized heavy rubberized suits, respirators, and lead/ceramic transport canisters are required to survive contamination and transport volatile items like Hell Slime.
